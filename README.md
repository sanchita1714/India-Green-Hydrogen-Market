The **India Green Hydrogen Market** is entering a transformative phase as the National Green Hydrogen Mission, renewable energy expansion, and industrial decarbonization initiatives stimulate investments across the hydrogen value chain. The government’s mission targets production of 5 million metric tons of green hydrogen annually by 2030, while supporting domestic electrolyser manufacturing and large-scale project development. Green hydrogen adoption is expanding across steel, refining, fertilizers, chemicals, transportation, and power generation, where companies are seeking alternatives to fossil-based hydrogen and other carbon-intensive energy sources. Advancements in alkaline, PEM, AEM, and solid oxide electrolysers, combined with renewable-energy integration and improved storage technologies, are further enhancing production efficiency and scalability.

The market is also creating significant opportunities across electrolyser manufacturing, renewable power integration, green ammonia and methanol, hydrogen storage, fueling infrastructure, and export-oriented hydrogen hubs. Solar PV is currently a major renewable source supporting green hydrogen production, while wind power is increasingly being integrated with electrolysers in resource-rich states such as Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, and Maharashtra. However, high production costs, limited hydrogen transportation and storage infrastructure, uncertain offtake arrangements, and evolving certification frameworks remain important challenges. With India targeting a stronger position in international hydrogen supply chains, opportunities are emerging for domestic and global companies to establish partnerships, develop large-scale projects, and supply green hydrogen and derivatives to markets including the European Union, Japan, and South Korea.

## Conclusion

The India Green Hydrogen Market is positioned for rapid, policy-driven expansion as renewable energy capacity, electrolyser innovation, industrial demand, and international collaboration reshape the country’s clean-energy ecosystem. Continued government incentives, declining renewable-energy costs, investments in hydrogen infrastructure, and growing demand from hard-to-abate industries are expected to strengthen the commercial viability of green hydrogen.
